From parts bin to finish line

Four moves, the same ones you would make at a real bench. Scroll and watch this robot come together.

  1. 1

    Build it

    Choose a chassis, a board, motors, a driver and a battery. Every part is a real one with real numbers: an L298N loses two volts, an N20 motor spins at 300 rpm.

  2. 2

    Wire it

    Give each wire a pin. BotBench catches what a real board would punish: a motor on a pin with no PWM, two parts sharing a pin, a camera fighting the SPI bus.

  3. 3

    Code it

    Write the sketch you would upload. setup(), loop(), digitalRead, analogWrite, pulseIn, Servo and Pixy2 all behave like the real thing, timing included.

  4. 4

    Run it and fix it

    Press Run. If the robot leaves the line, the run stops on the spot, the coach explains what your code did wrong and offers a version that works.

Why I made this

        
I built BotBench because I kept frying motor drivers. My first line follower cost me two L298Ns, a bag of jumper wires and most of a weekend, and the actual bug was one swapped pin.

        
Most of what goes wrong with a small robot is in the wiring and the code: a motor on a pin that can't do PWM, a sensor that reads HIGH when you expected LOW, a delay() that makes the loop too slow to steer. You can catch all of that before you plug in a battery.

        
So this is the bench I wanted back then. The parts act like the ones in my drawer, quirks included, and a failed run tells you what happened. It isn't real physics down to the last gram. If you find a case where your robot does something here that it wouldn't do on the floor, I'd like to hear about it.

        
— Prisha Patel

      

    


    

      
Questions people ask

      

        

          Is it free?
          
Yes. There's no paid version and nothing locked behind one.

        

        

          Do I need an account?
          
No. Open the workbench and start. Your robot, arena and code are saved in this browser, so they'll be there next time on the same device. Make a free account if you want to keep more than one robot, open them on another computer, or get your times on the challenge boards. You can sign up with an email or with Google, GitHub or Apple.

        

        

          Does my code run on a real Arduino?
          
It's meant to. You write normal Arduino C++ with setup(), loop(), pinMode, analogWrite, pulseIn, Servo and so on, then copy it into the Arduino IDE and upload. The simulator covers the subset most robot sketches use. Classes, structs and pointers aren't supported yet, and it will tell you if you use one.

        

        

          Which boards and parts are there?
          
Boards: Arduino Uno, Nano, Mega 2560 and ESP32 DevKit. Motors: TT gear motors, N20 and 25GA-370. Drivers: L298N, L293D and TB6612FNG. Sensors: TCRT5000, 3 and 5-channel IR arrays, HC-SR04 ultrasonic and the Pixy2 camera. Plus an SG90 gripper, a few chassis and the usual battery packs.

        

        

          Does it work offline, or on my phone?
          
Both. After your first visit the site is cached, so it opens without a connection. You can also add it to your home screen. It runs on a phone, though writing code is a lot nicer with a keyboard and a bigger screen.

        

        

          Why did my robot fail when the code looks right?
          
Usually it's the hardware side. The motor is on a pin without PWM, the left and right wires are swapped, the driver's standby pin is never set HIGH, or the batteries are too weak once the L298N takes its 2 V. Sometimes the loop is just too slow because of a long delay() or a lot of Serial.print. When a run fails, the coach points at the spot on the track and tells you which of these it was.
